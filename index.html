Robot: Pain d' Epi
Scoring (cone / cube)
End effector receives cones from the floor intake in a set orientation. Grip design unconfirmed (see CAD).
Intake
Floor intake for cubes and cones. The beta robot had a cube-only floor intake; the cone roller was added later. The cone layout rotates the 3 in polycarbonate roller about the intake pivot and adds a 2 in flex wheel shaft that spins opposite the other rollers. Surgical tubing pulls the 2 in roller outward; when a cone is in far enough the rollers lock and the 2 in roller pivots about the 3 in roller to a hard stop. The team states another motor would have put the robot over the weight limit.
Arm / elevator
Tilted elevator, adapted from a 2022 off-season project elevator. That project tested a belted elevator; the team chose Dyneema instead because it was easier to package, routed through the tubes, and the belt gearbox took more space.
Drivetrain
Swerve: the team lists swerve offsets among possible causes of the same playoff failure. Module type unconfirmed (see CAD).
Endgame
Unconfirmed (see CAD)
Auto
The team added cone floor pickup so auto could place 2 cones and a cube for a high link, instead of a preloaded cone and two cubes. They expected many Championship matches to tie at 193 points and be decided by auto. They state supercharging later reduced the value of this. At Central Valley, a practice-match auto failed; AdvantageKit log replay showed a wrong heading target, traced to how the code flipped it.
Vision
AprilTag vision: the team lists it among possible causes when the robot lost control in a division playoff match. Camera count and software unconfirmed (see code).
Why study it
PICK (CAD + code): rank 3 of 3290 on Local EPA, with public Onshape CAD and code and team replies that explain the design method. Study three things. First, the CAD workflow: all layout sketches live in one Onshape document and are derived into each subsystem document. Second, the cone intake: a 2 in flex wheel roller, held out by surgical tubing, pivots about the 3 in polycarbonate roller once the rollers lock, so the cone reaches the end effector in the right orientation without another motor. Third, the software practice: the team used AdvantageKit log replay to find a flipped heading target in auto at Central Valley and a missing gyro signal during Curie playoffs.
Lessons for students
  • Keep all layout sketches in one Onshape document and derive them into each subsystem document.
  • Try passive geometry before adding a motor: locked rollers make the cone roller pivot and orient the cone for the handoff.
  • Off-season projects can feed the season: a 2022 project elevator went onto the 2023 robot, mounted tilted, which the team says helped them build quickly.
  • Base mechanism scope on match math: the team added cone pickup expecting tied Championship matches to be decided by auto points.
  • Log everything and replay it: at Curie playoffs the logs showed no gyro data, which let the electrical team fix the robot before the next match.
Evidence and confidence
High confidence. Public Onshape CAD and code, with team replies that include layout-sketch screenshots, the cone intake geometry and two debugging examples from log replay. Rank 3 of 3290 on Local EPA. Released 2023-12-05 on Chief Delphi.

Robot: UPPERCUT
Scoring (cone / cube)
End effector type unconfirmed (see the binder). The robot had no colour sensor; the driver set cone or cube mode (see intake). LEDs flash white when a game piece is acquired. This tells the driver the robot holds it when the intake is out of view.
Intake
Intake with side rollers and a top roller. The driver chose cone or cube mode, because the side roller speeds and the top roller position differed by game piece. In cone mode a beam break sensor detected the cone and the intake clamped onto it automatically. In cube mode the rollers stopped when the cube hit a bumper switch at the back of the intake. The OP names 'single substation floor pickups' as a case where the driver cannot see the intake or the game piece.
Arm / elevator
Arm on a shoulder pivot; layout details unconfirmed (see the binder). Gas shocks went over centre to hold the shoulder pivot in the starting configuration. The top pivot point attaches to 1/8 in box beam. On the practice bot a crack formed there and the tube wall deformed. The team added 1/4 in aluminum plates with four #8 bolts. The OP names Loctite 609 as the retaining compound used to remove backlash.
Drivetrain
Swerve. The team released STL covers for its swerve modules, and the file name names MK4i. The covers kept foreign material out; the team removed the top cover to inspect each module before and after each event. To grease a module, the team put grease on the end of a zip tie, fed it into the gearbox with the middle cover on, and turned the wheels. A Pigeon 2 gyro showed no noticeable drift over a match.

Unconfirmed (not stated in the public release).
Auto
Routines not described in the thread. The OP reports that Phoenix Pro time sync improved the accuracy and repeatability of the odometry. Autonomous Award at the Archimedes Division and at ONT District Newmarket.

One Limelight in the drivetrain, angled up to track cone nodes and AprilTags. No game piece detection in auto or teleop; the team states it did not need it.

Robot: Phantom
Scoring (cone / cube)
The robot scores on all three levels (reveal post). A team reply describes the cone sitting in the intake and the robot ejecting it to score, so the intake also acts as the end effector. Roller and motor detail: Unconfirmed (see CAD).
Intake
Picks up cones and cubes from the ground and the shelf, including fallen-over cones (reveal post). A Time of Flight sensor on the inner wall of the intake measures where the cone sits. Mechanism detail: Unconfirmed (see CAD).
Arm / elevator
2-stage cascade-rigged telescoping arm (reveal post) on a shoulder pivot (team reply). Green cord on pulleys on the first stage drives the second stage from the first stage's motion (OP reply). A team reply names an A-frame structure with a dead-axle main pivot as part of the low-backlash design. Joints use only the integrated Falcon encoders, with REV ION gears for a tight fit on hex shafts (team reply).
Drivetrain
SDS Mk4i L3 swerve on a 26x28 frame perimeter (reveal post). A team reply states three gearings were run in 2023: standard L3, L3 with a 16T pinion, and L3 with an 18T pinion. The robot weighs about 90 lbs without ballast and is under 17 in tall when stowed.
Endgame
Auto balancing on the Charge Station (reveal post). Dock and engage method: Unconfirmed (see code).
Auto
3 game piece autonomous and auto balancing, listed as separate features (reveal post). Whether one routine does both: Unconfirmed. Paths come from PathPlanner. The team did not use the PathPlanner marker system; custom code runs commands in parallel or in sequence with path following (code thread).
Vision
Two angled cameras, used constantly in teleop to relocalize the robot on the field (team reply). Camera model and coprocessor: Unconfirmed (see code).
Why study it
PICK (CAD + binder): Local EPA rank 6 of 3290 (69-16-0), Pacific Northwest District Championship winner, with Onshape CAD, a STEP and Parasolid export, a tech binder and a separate code release. Study the compact arm package: a 26x28 frame under 17 in tall when stowed, about 90 lbs without ballast, with a 2-stage telescoping arm whose second stage is driven by cord fixed to the base stage and run over pulleys on the first stage. Team replies explain the anti-tip sequencing (extension waits for the shoulder) and the backlash choices: Falcon integrated encoders only, REV ION gears for a tight hex fit, and a dead-axle main pivot. Team replies also describe a Time of Flight cone-alignment routine and a feedforward tuning procedure.
Lessons for students
  • Sequence arm joints to limit tipping: the code does not extend the arm until the shoulder is within an allowed distance of its setpoint.
  • A telescoping second stage can be driven without its own motor: cord fixed to the base stage runs over pulleys on stage one and pulls stage two.
  • Low backlash without absolute encoders: the team used only Falcon integrated encoders, REV ION gears with a tight hex fit, and a dead-axle main pivot.
  • Driver-assisted scoring: a Time of Flight sensor finds the cone center, the robot PIDs to the nearest column, and it ejects only when X, Y, yaw and pitch pass strict thresholds. The team reports the driver was often faster.
  • Path-following feedforward can be tuned by hand: the team turns off path PID, tunes velocity feedforward on a slow straight path, then acceleration feedforward, and repeated this for each of three drive gearings.
Evidence and confidence
High confidence. Public Onshape CAD, a STEP and Parasolid export, a tech binder PDF and a public code repository. Team replies in the release threads explain the arm sequencing, encoder and gear choices, auto-alignment and path-following tuning. Released 2023-05-05 on Chief Delphi.

Why study it
PICK (CAD): rank 5 of 3290 on Local EPA, District Championship Winner at the FIRST In Texas District Championship and Championship Division Finalist in Galileo. Study one mechanism: the arm gearbox mount. The gearbox is a slip fit on the aluminum cross tube at the base of the tower, held axially by collars, and 2 turnbuckles pivot it around the tube to tension the chain. The team says the method comes from heavy equipment practice, makes it easier to keep the shafts parallel during adjustment, typically uses fewer and simpler parts than a linear adjuster, and allows side-to-side play for chain tracking. They chose it because they expected to retension often under the load of a fast arm. The CAD is the working model; the team says some parts in it do not match the robot.
Lessons for students
  • Tension a chain by pivoting the whole gearbox around a round tube, set with 2 turnbuckles. The team took this from heavy equipment practice: it makes it easier to keep the shafts parallel, and rotational motion typically needs fewer and simpler parts than a linear adjuster.
  • One cross tube does two jobs: it is structure for the tower and the pivot for the gearbox. The team says they wanted the cross member for structure anyway.
  • The team expected to adjust chain tension often because of the force from the fast arm, so the adjustment is part of the mount.
  • The release is the working model, not a cleaned-up copy. The team presents it as an example of a well-organized but practical FRC CAD model.

Robot: Tsunami
Scoring (cone / cube)
At the season events the robot used a v4b with an intake. Changed before Chezy Champs: the v4b and intake were replaced with a 1323-style arm and wrist, built in the team's own style with the same roller locations. The new arm and wrist are made from 7075 and light weighted to reduce mass.

Intake plates are 7075-T6 aluminum; the team says the plates took big hits without damage, though a front roller snapped. Rollers are 1.25 in polycarbonate tube with 1 in silicone tubing stretched over it, with Markforged-printed hubs glued in, on 3/8 in ThunderHex. The team added star wheels after seeing a pair on 1323's intake. The intake pivot runs on a single Falcon at about 30:1 in Motion Magic Voltage mode.
Arm / elevator
Slanted elevator with WCP inline elevator blocks and a tensioned belt last stage. The team names worn constant force springs as the only major elevator issue; it was tensioned once on the competition robot. Before Chezy Champs the team added ballast low on the back of the elevator to prevent tipping.
Drivetrain
Drivetrain type is not stated by the team. The Behind the Bumpers summary mentions custom swerve modifications; module type unconfirmed (not stated in the public release).

Unconfirmed (not stated in the public release).
Auto

Vision
Changed before Chezy Champs: a tower was added to hold a Limelight for more reliable retro tape tracking, plus a rear-facing Limelight for cone tracking in auto. The team says a rear camera was hard to fit on the low side of the slanted elevator before the change. Season vision setup unconfirmed.
Why study it

Lessons for students

PICK (CAD + code): 'BB Mantis', released as STEP CAD and LabVIEW code, with separate releases of the team's 3D-printed swerve module and TPU suspension tread. Study it as a 3D-printing case. Lessons for students
