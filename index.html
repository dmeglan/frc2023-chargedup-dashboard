Tilted Dyneema elevator; cube/cone floor intake whose spring-loaded cone roller sets the handoff orientation
74
Local EPA
Yes
USA-CA · 40-9 · #3
Scoring (cone / cube)
End effector receives cones from the floor intake in a set orientation.
Intake
Floor intake for cubes and cones. The beta robot had a cube-only floor intake; the cone roller was added later. The cone layout rotates the 3 in polycarbonate roller about the intake pivot and adds a 2 in flex wheel shaft that spins opposite the other rollers. Surgical tubing pulls the 2 in roller outward; when a cone is in far enough the rollers lock and the 2 in roller pivots about the 3 in roller to a hard stop. The team states another motor would have put the robot over the weight limit.
Arm / elevator
Tilted elevator, adapted from a 2022 off-season project elevator. That project tested a belted elevator; the team chose Dyneema instead because it was easier to package, routed through the tubes, and the belt gearbox took more space.
Drivetrain
Swerve: the team lists swerve offsets among possible causes of the same playoff failure.
Endgame
Unconfirmed
Auto
The team added cone floor pickup so auto could place 2 cones and a cube for a high link, instead of a preloaded cone and two cubes. They expected many Championship matches to tie at 193 points and be decided by auto. They state supercharging later reduced the value of this. At Central Valley, a practice-match auto failed; AdvantageKit log replay showed a wrong heading target, traced to how the code flipped it.
Vision
AprilTag vision: the team lists it among possible causes when the robot lost control in a division playoff match.
Why study it
Study three things: the Onshape layout workflow, the passive cone intake, and AdvantageKit log replay. All layout sketches live in one Onshape document and are derived into each subsystem document. Log replay showed a flipped heading target in auto at Central Valley and a missing gyro signal during Curie playoffs.

  • Log everything and replay it: the replay let the electrical team fix the robot before the next Curie playoff match.

★ OP Robotics · UPPERCUT
Arm with gas-shock shoulder hold; driver-set cone/cube intake modes; upward Limelight; MK4i swerve
73
Local EPA
Partial
Canada · 61-8 · #4
Scoring (cone / cube)
The driver set cone or cube mode; the robot had no colour sensor. LEDs flash white when a game piece is acquired. This tells the driver the robot holds it when the intake is out of view.
Intake
Intake with side rollers and a top roller. The driver chose cone or cube mode, because the side roller speeds and the top roller position differed by game piece. In cone mode a beam break sensor detected the cone and the intake clamped onto it automatically. In cube mode the rollers stopped when the cube hit a bumper switch at the back of the intake. The OP names 'single substation floor pickups' as a case where the driver cannot see the intake or the game piece.
Arm / elevator
Arm on a shoulder pivot. Gas shocks went over centre to hold the shoulder pivot in the starting configuration. The top pivot point attaches to 1/8 in box beam. On the practice bot a crack formed there and the tube wall deformed. The team added 1/4 in aluminum plates with four #8 bolts. The OP names Loctite 609 as the retaining compound used to remove backlash.
Drivetrain
Swerve. The team released STL covers for its swerve modules, and the file name names MK4i. The covers kept foreign material out; the team removed the top cover to inspect each module before and after each event. To grease a module, the team put grease on the end of a zip tie, fed it into the gearbox with the middle cover on, and turned the wheels. A Pigeon 2 gyro showed no noticeable drift over a match.
Endgame
Unconfirmed
Auto
The OP reports that Phoenix Pro time sync improved the accuracy and repeatability of the odometry. Autonomous Award at the Archimedes Division and at ONT District Newmarket.
Vision
One Limelight in the drivetrain, angled up to track cone nodes and AprilTags. No game piece detection in auto or teleop; the team states it did not need it.
Why study it
Read the technical binder with the OP's thread answers, which record design and field fixes. Study the driver-set intake modes, the gas-shock shoulder hold and the reinforced pivot mount. District Championship Winner at the FIRST Ontario Provincial Championship; Autonomous Award at the Archimedes Division.
Lessons for students
  • Use the practice robot to find structural failures, as the team did with the top pivot mount.
Evidence and confidence
High confidence. OP answers in the release thread on structure, sensors, intake logic and vision, posted from 2023-05 to 2025-10. Released 2023-05-24 on Chief Delphi.

★ Jack in the Bot · Phantom
Shoulder-pivot 2-stage cascade-rigged telescoping arm; floor and shelf pickup; SDS Mk4i L3 swerve, 26x28 frame
73
Local EPA
Yes
USA-WA · 69-16 · #6
Scoring (cone / cube)
The robot scores on all three levels. The cone sits in the intake and the robot ejects it to score, so the intake also acts as the end effector.
Intake
Picks up cones and cubes from the ground and the shelf, including fallen-over cones. A Time of Flight sensor on the inner wall of the intake measures where the cone sits.
Arm / elevator
2-stage cascade-rigged telescoping arm on a shoulder pivot. Cord on pulleys on the first stage drives the second stage from the first stage's motion. An A-frame structure with a dead-axle main pivot is part of the low-backlash design. Joints use only the integrated Falcon encoders, with REV ION gears for a tight fit on hex shafts.
Drivetrain
SDS Mk4i L3 swerve on a 26x28 frame perimeter. Three gearings were run: standard L3, L3 with a 16T pinion, and L3 with an 18T pinion. The robot weighs about 90 lbs without ballast and is under 17 in tall when stowed.
Endgame
Auto balancing on the Charge Station.
Auto
3 game piece autonomous and auto balancing, listed as separate features. Paths come from PathPlanner. The team did not use the PathPlanner marker system; custom code runs commands in parallel or in sequence with path following.
Vision
Two angled cameras, used constantly in teleop to relocalize the robot on the field. Camera model and coprocessor: Unconfirmed.
Why study it
Study the compact arm package and how it moves fast without tipping the robot. Team replies explain the anti-tip sequencing, the backlash choices, a Time of Flight cone-alignment routine and a feedforward tuning procedure. Pacific Northwest District Championship winner.
Lessons for students
  • Sequence arm joints to limit tipping: the code does not extend the arm until the shoulder is within an allowed distance of its setpoint.
  • A telescoping stage can be driven without its own motor; trace the cord and pulleys that drive stage two.
  • Driver-assisted scoring: the robot PIDs to the nearest column, and it ejects only when X, Y, yaw and pitch pass strict thresholds. The team reports the driver was often faster.
  • Path-following feedforward can be tuned by hand: the team turns off path PID, tunes velocity feedforward on a slow straight path, then acceleration feedforward, and repeated this for each of the drive gearings.
Evidence and confidence
High confidence. Team replies in the release threads explain the arm sequencing, encoder and gear choices, auto-alignment and path-following tuning. Released 2023-05-05 on Chief Delphi.

Lessons for students
  • The team took the pivoting gearbox mount from heavy equipment practice: it makes it easier to keep the shafts parallel, and rotational motion typically needs fewer and simpler parts than a linear adjuster.

  • The team expected to adjust chain tension often because of the force from the fast arm, so the adjustment is part of the mount.
  • The team presents the working model as an example of a well-organized but practical FRC CAD model.

HighTide · Tsunami
Slanted elevator with a v4b and intake; before Chezy Champs changed to a 1323-style arm and wrist

USA-CA · 60-10-1 · #8
Scoring (cone / cube)
At season events: a v4b with an intake. Before Chezy Champs the v4b and intake were replaced with a 1323-style arm and wrist, in the team's own style with the same roller locations. The new arm and wrist are 7075, light weighted to reduce mass.
Intake
Intake plates are 7075-T6 aluminum; the plates took big hits without damage, though a front roller snapped. Rollers are 1.25 in polycarbonate tube with 1 in silicone tubing stretched over it, with Markforged-printed hubs glued in, on 3/8 in ThunderHex. The team added star wheels after seeing a pair on 1323's intake. The intake pivot runs on a single Falcon at about 30:1 in Motion Magic Voltage mode.
Arm / elevator
Slanted elevator with WCP inline elevator blocks and a tensioned belt last stage. The team names worn constant force springs as the only major elevator issue; it was tensioned once. Before Chezy Champs the team added ballast low on the back of the elevator to prevent tipping.
Drivetrain
The Behind the Bumpers summary mentions custom swerve modifications.
Endgame
Unconfirmed
Auto
In season the robot scored two mid cones and one high cone in auto; after the Chezy Champs changes, two high cones without tipping. The Behind the Bumpers summary mentions on-the-fly dynamic trajectories. Autonomous Award at Ventura County.
Vision
Changed before Chezy Champs: a tower was added to hold a Limelight for more reliable retro tape tracking, plus a rear-facing Limelight for cone tracking in auto. The team says a rear camera was hard to fit on the low side of the slanted elevator before the change.
Why study it
Simple position control: one Falcon per position subsystem, typically shimmed hex gears, and belts only on the last elevator stage. Championship Winner.
Lessons for students
  • Mechanical stiffness reduces tuning work: the team says PID effort approaches zero when a mechanism is light and has low backlash.
  • Hex gear backlash can be removed with .002 in shim stock tape wrapped around 1-3 faces of the bore; the team posted photos of the process.
  • On position subsystems the team prefers well-shimmed gears and chain to belts.
  • The team builds without a lathe, cutting shafts on a cold saw and spacing them with COTS spacers and standoffs.
Evidence and confidence
Medium confidence. Later team posts in the reveal thread with design detail and photos, plus a Behind the Bumpers pit tour video. The team is hesitant to share even side screenshots of the intake geometry. Revealed 2023-03-01 on Chief Delphi.

★ WildStang
Lift, belt-driven arm and wrist with a cone-wedging gripper; double substation loading; REV MAXSwerve
70
Local EPA
Yes
USA-IL · 38-5-1 · #16
Scoring (cone / cube)
A gripper on a wrist scores cones and cubes. The gripper wedges on the funnel shape of a cone. After a cone trap in a Seven Rivers match, the team added a plastic closeout plate to the front of the robot. The team reports 11-12 teleop cycles after its Championship upgrades, scoring on the middle set of links.
Intake
Loads at the double substation from the human player. Before the Championship, the team reviewed its season match video and found that substation pickup time was its biggest slowdown. Changed for the Championship: cones were fed on their side, an idea the team credits to team 324. The scoring auto-align code was reused to set the pickup pose at the substation, with the lift partly extended as a crumple zone.
Arm / elevator
A lift with constant force springs, a belt-driven arm and a wrist with an encoder. The arm is backlash sensitive and uses MAX Planetary gearboxes. A wrist encoder broke from an impact at Seven Rivers; the team then supported the encoder axle on both ends. Rigging capstans are 3D prints sleeved in aluminum so rigging tension does not crush them.
Drivetrain
REV MAXSwerve. The team bought five modules in fall 2022 because SDS MK4i supply was uncertain; it ran SDS MK3 in 2021 and 2022. The drivetrain used NEOs. The team zeros modules in the REV fixture with only the bevel fork installed, and machined clearance pockets into the fixture.
Endgame
The team mentions balancing at the end of a Seven Rivers playoff match.
Auto
Added before the Championship: a 3-piece auto on the bump side, which kept it compatible with 7-game-piece alliance autos. The team uses its own path follower, which takes velocity, acceleration and direction from the path and handles rotation separately. TBA lists the Autonomous Award at the Seven Rivers Regional.
Vision
Two Limelight cameras in a 'cross-eyed' layout. The second camera widened the field of view so a nearby AprilTag was always in sight, instead of relying on wheel odometry. Changed for the Championship: both cameras were turned up to see the loading station tag, about 35 deg cross-eyed and 10 deg above the horizon.
Why study it
Study how the team used match video to rebuild its loading cycle between events. The team found double substation pickup time was its biggest slowdown. For the Championship it changed how cones were fed, how the robot set its pickup pose, and the Limelight layout.
Lessons for students
  • Use the shape of the game piece: the team says a cone fed on its side gives the gripper a larger target.
  • Check the field of view at the scoring spots, where one camera would lose a nearby AprilTag.
  • Split the controls by role: the driver chose when each robot action happened, and the operator chose what that action did. A team member says other students picked up driving easily at the fall driver tryouts.
Evidence and confidence
High confidence. The team's build blog links the code; its posts describe the drivetrain, the camera layout, an added auto and the rebuild before the Championship. CAD link announced 2024-02-05 in the team's Chief Delphi build blog.
